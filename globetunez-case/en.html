Case 03 · Partner project

I built a music platform with radio, talent shows and tickets for a Nigerian music scene

Built mobile-first for low-end Android phones and unstable networks, with two AI agents as development partners working under written rules.

01 · The brief

One digital home for a music scene that already exists in the real world

Blaze It Up Radio Africa and 9thwunder are an existing grassroots scene in Nigeria: a radio station, talent shows with audience voting, physical concerts and a magazine. The host already receives plenty of audition videos from artists and runs competitions where the crowd votes at the event itself. All of it ran on loose channels, with no single place where fans and artists could meet between events.

The brief was to build that place without replacing the physical side: an app that extends an event before, during and after. Fans listen to the radio, discover artists, watch and vote on auditions, buy tickets and can join the club. Artists submit auditions, get a profile, followers and a path to the next opportunity. The organiser moderates, opens and closes votes, scans tickets at the door and reads the results.

The constraints were hard from day one: many users have low-end Android phones, expensive data and unstable connections. So the app is an installable PWA with no app store, one webfont, unmistakable actions such as LISTEN LIVE, VOTE and BUY TICKET, and a rule that every network action needs a loading state, a success state and a safe failure state with a retry.

02 · How it fits together

Permissions in the database, money in a ledger

A modular monolith: one Next.js app against one Postgres database, where access control lives as Row Level Security inside the database itself and is tested with pgTAP. The frontend never hides anything as a security measure; the database decides.

LedgerStripe/Paystack webhooks → ticket or membership

Adminmoderation, results, door scanning

Payments follow one rule: the ledger is the truth. A webhook from Stripe or Paystack is verified by signature, reference, amount, currency and product before the app issues anything, and the same event can never issue twice. Real money cannot flow at all until a human opens a manual gate; until then every purchase is simulated and labelled as such.

Taken from the production site on 5 October 2026. This is the public part anyone can see; the events and competitions are the ones that were actually in the database that day.

Behind the scenes: admin

The admin area is built for one organiser on a phone, not an analyst at a desk. It shows only what needs a decision, and every action that touches money or publication explains itself before it runs. Screenshots from it are deliberately left out, because they show the pilot's account counts and test payments.

  • Waiting on youThe dashboard opens with three cards: auditions to approve or reject, artist applications and payment disputes. If a number cannot be read, it says "couldn't read this just now" instead of showing a zero.
  • Events and money kept apartEach event has its own numbers. Naira and dollars are always shown separately and never added together, and test payments sit in their own clearly labelled rows.
  • Switches with consequencesOne page with every switch: all payments, ticket sales, donations, festival mode, member passes, video on live sessions. The money switches ask you to type a word first, and every change is recorded with who made it.
  • Stats measured on real phonesThe app's opening time is measured on users' own phones, the first time the app loads, on the connections they actually have. The page shows the median and the "slowest 1 in 20" and says plainly that signed-out visitors are not counted.
  • Door scanning without signalTickets and member passes are scanned at the door with a code that needs the network only once. After that the scanner works offline, and the list reconciles when the signal is back.
  • Failures as a page, not an emailServer errors are collected on an admin page with code and message, so a production failure can be read by whoever runs the app, without access to logs.

05 · The way of working

Two AI agents as developers, under written rules

The code was written by Claude Code and Codex, which took turns implementing and reviewing each other's work. My role was product decisions, approvals and everything that touches production: applying migrations, verifying in the hosted database and taking the human gates the agents are not allowed to open themselves.

It worked because the rules were written down in the repository. Documentation is part of the product: a change without an updated TODO, handover, changelog and decision log is not finished. The numbers show it. The decision log holds more than 240 numbered architecture decisions, and the changelog, decision log and handover together run to over a megabyte of text.

Read before you write

Before a code change the agent reads the product, scope, architecture, security and decision documents. Nobody may swap the framework, database, payment or streaming provider without a concrete problem, alternatives and human approval.

Reviews found real bugs

The review rounds between the two agents found, among other things, a race condition where two concurrent approvals could overwrite each other, and an approval that could demote an admin. Both were fixed and proven with two genuinely concurrent database connections, not just reasoned about.

Numbers a machine recomputes

Until 24 August every check ran only when somebody remembered, on one laptop. CI was introduced after the changelog had claimed 85 test files where there were 83. Now every number is recomputed on every push, and this case uses only numbers from the latest green run.

Failures in the field, not in theory

When a tester in Nigeria could not receive SMS codes, the incident was diagnosed read-only in production, documented with timestamps and error codes, and only then fixed in the app's error copy and phone-number handling. No guessed fixes were switched on.

07 · Limits and trade-offs

What the platform deliberately is not yet

Not launched publicly

The app runs in a closed pilot with testers. That is why there are no user counts, vote counts or revenue here, and why the screenshots show only the public part. Admin is described in text, not shown.

No real money without approval

Tickets and memberships are built against Stripe and Paystack but sit behind a manual gate. Donations and memberships in naira are not built. Payouts to artists are manual and reviewed, never automatic.

No livestream provider

Live participation (vote, request a song, follow the current performer) is built, but the video itself awaits a choice between Mux, Cloudflare Stream and Amazon IVS after a rehearsal on a real network in Nigeria.

Community deferred

A broad social feed with posts, comments and messages was deliberately left out of the first version. The core is the chain from audition to vote to event to support, not yet another social network.
