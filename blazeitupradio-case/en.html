Case 03 · Partner project

I built a music platform with radio, talent shows and tickets for a Nigerian music scene

Blaze It Up Radio Africa is a radio station and a music scene in Nigeria. The app brings what the scene already does into one place: listen to the station, watch and vote on the talent show's auditions, buy tickets to the concerts, follow the artists and support them through a members' club. It is built for low-end Android phones and unstable networks, and developed together with two AI agents under written rules.

01 · The brief

One digital home for a music scene that already exists in the real world

Blaze It Up Radio Africa is a radio station, and 9thwunder is the brand behind it: talent shows, concerts, a magazine and a fan club, run from Lagos with an audience in Nigeria and in the American diaspora. It already exists in the real world. The host already receives audition videos from artists, runs competitions where the crowd votes at the show itself, and sells tickets to the concerts. But all of it ran on loose channels such as WhatsApp, Instagram and an older website, with no single place where fans and artists could meet between two events.

The brief was to build that place without replacing the physical side: an app that makes an event bigger before, during and after. Fans listen to the radio, discover artists, watch and vote on auditions, buy a ticket and can join Club 9thwunder, a members' club where part of the fee goes directly to one artist the member chooses. Artists submit an audition, get a profile and followers, and a path to the next opportunity. The organiser approves auditions, opens and closes votes, scans tickets at the door and reads the results.

The constraints were hard from day one: many users have low-end Android phones, expensive mobile data and a network that drops. So the app is a PWA, a web app installed straight from the browser with no app store, with a single font to download, big unmistakable buttons such as LISTEN LIVE, VOTE and BUY TICKET, and one fixed rule: every time the app talks to the server, the user must be able to see that it is working, that it succeeded, or that it failed and can be retried.

02 · How it fits together

Permissions in the database, money in a ledger

The app is built as one program (a modular monolith) on top of one database. Who may see and change what is decided down in the database with Row Level Security, that is, permissions per row, and it is tested automatically with pgTAP. Something being hidden on screen never counts as security; the database says no regardless.

Phonethe app installed from the browser; an offline page if the network drops
→
Next.js serverperforms actions on the user's behalf and checks the sign-in on every request
→
Postgres databasepermissions per row (Row Level Security), 105 numbered changes
→
LedgerStripe and Paystack report payments; only then is a ticket or membership issued
→
Adminapproving auditions, results, scanning at the door

Payments follow one rule: the ledger is the truth. The ledger is the app's own list of every single payment, and nothing is issued until a payment is in it. When Stripe or Paystack reports a payment (a so-called webhook), the app checks the sender's signature, the order reference, amount, currency and product before writing anything into the ledger, and the same message can never issue two tickets. Real money cannot move at all until a human has switched payments on with a manual switch; until then every purchase is simulated and clearly labelled as a test.

03 · The stack

Three layers, and why it is built this way

Most software is split into three layers: what the user sees, the rules that decide what may happen, and the data that has to be right no matter what. Here are the layers, the providers the app talks to, and the reason for each choice.

What we use, and why

ToolWhat it isWhy it was chosen
TypeScriptThe programming language across the whole app; JavaScript with types, so mistakes are caught before the code runs.One language for both screen and server, and the types make it safer to let AI agents write and change large amounts of code.
React and Next.js 16React draws the screens; Next.js is the framework around it that also runs the server side and builds the pages.The most widespread choice for this kind of app, so answers exist for everything, and Next.js can perform actions on the server without a separate API project.
Tailwind CSSA tool for looks and layout, written as small classes directly in the screen code.Fast to keep consistent across 60 screens, and unused styles are removed automatically, so the app stays small to download.
PWAProgressive Web App: a website that can be installed on the phone's home screen and partly work without a network.No app store, no waiting for approval, and it takes almost no space on a low-end Android phone.
Postgres via SupabasePostgres is a relational database (tables with rows and rules). Supabase hosts it and adds sign-in and file storage on top.Permissions can be enforced in the database itself, so no mistake on screen can expose someone else's data. Supabase saves a small team an entire operations department.
pgTAPTests that run inside the database and try to read or write as an anonymous visitor, a signed-in user and an admin.It is the only way to prove that the permissions hold, whatever the app or an attacker sends.
Vitest and PlaywrightVitest tests the app's code piece by piece; Playwright clicks through the app in a real browser.Fast tests catch logic errors in seconds; browser tests catch what a user would actually hit, such as a button that cannot be tapped on mobile.

Behind the scenes: admin

The admin area is built for one organiser on a phone, not an analyst at a desk. It shows only what needs a decision, and every action that touches money or publication explains itself before it runs. Screenshots from it are deliberately left out, because they show the pilot's account counts and test payments.

  • Waiting on youThe dashboard opens with three cards: auditions to approve or reject, artist applications and payment disputes. If a number cannot be read, it says "couldn't read this just now" instead of showing a zero.
  • Events and money kept apartEach event has its own numbers. Naira and dollars are always shown separately and never added together, and test payments sit in their own clearly labelled rows.
  • Switches with consequencesOne page with every switch: all payments, ticket sales, donations, festival mode, member passes, video on live sessions. The money switches ask you to type a word first, and every change is saved with the name of whoever pressed it.
  • Stats measured on real phonesThe app's opening time is measured on users' own phones, the first time the app loads, on the connections they actually have. The page shows the median and the "slowest 1 in 20" and says plainly that signed-out visitors are not counted.
  • Door scanning without signalTickets and member passes are scanned at the door with a code that needs the network only once. After that the scanner works offline, and the list reconciles when the signal is back.
  • Failures as a page, not an emailServer errors are collected on an admin page with code and message, so a production failure can be read by whoever runs the app, without access to logs.

05 · The way of working

Two AI agents as developers, under written rules

The code was written by two AI tools, Claude Code and Codex, which took turns building and reviewing each other's work. My role was to make the product decisions, approve each step and do everything that touches real operations: put database changes into production, check that they work, and take the decisions only a human may take, such as switching real payments on.

It worked because the rules were written down next to the code. Documentation is part of the product: a change is not finished until the task list, the handover note, the changelog and the decision log are updated. The numbers show it. The decision log holds more than 240 written decisions about why something is built the way it is, and the three logbooks together run to over a megabyte of text.

Read before you write

Before a code change the agent reads the documents on the product, the scope, the architecture, security and earlier decisions. Nobody may swap a technology or provider without a concrete problem, written alternatives and approval from a human.

Reviews found real bugs

The reviews between the two agents found, among other things, a race condition: two administrators approving at the same time could overwrite each other. They also found an approval that could accidentally strip an admin of their rights. Both were fixed and proven with two genuinely concurrent database connections, not just explained away.

Numbers a machine recomputes

Until 24 August every check ran only when somebody remembered, on one laptop. The automated checks were introduced after the changelog had claimed 85 test files where there were 83. Now every number is recomputed every time code is pushed, and this case uses only numbers from the latest green run.

Failures in the field, not in theory

When a tester in Nigeria could not receive SMS codes, the incident was first examined in production without changing anything, documented with timestamps and error codes, and only then fixed in the app's error copy and phone-number handling. No guessed fixes were switched on.

07 · Limits and trade-offs

What the platform deliberately is not yet

Not launched publicly

The app runs in a closed pilot with testers. That is why there are no user counts, vote counts or revenue here, and why the screenshots show only the public part. Admin is described in text, not shown.

No real money without approval

Tickets and memberships are built against Stripe and Paystack, but real payments require a human to switch them on. Donations and memberships in naira are not built yet. Payouts to artists are manual and reviewed, never automatic.

No livestream provider

Participation during a live show (vote, request a song, follow the current performer) is built, but the video stream itself awaits a choice of provider, Mux, Cloudflare Stream or Amazon IVS, after a rehearsal on a real mobile network in Nigeria.

Community deferred

A broad social feed with posts, comments and messages was deliberately left out of the first version. The core is the chain from audition to vote to concert to support, not yet another social network.
